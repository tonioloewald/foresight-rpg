# Expected Pursuit rule (reconstructed from the four pages only)

## The rule I would expect

**Shape.** A pursuit is an opposed task repeated each round, tracking a single number, the **Lead**.

- **Lead scale:** 0 = contact, 1–2 = close (you can attack or grab), 3–5 = medium, 6–8 = long, 9 = nearly out of sight, 10 = lost.
- **Starting Lead:** 5 by default; 3 if the quarry bolts from under your nose; 7 with a real head start.
- **Round length:** about 10 seconds on foot, about 30 seconds in vehicles, half a day across country. The GM sets the tempo; nobody tracks the clock.

**Each round:**

1. **Initiative.** Roll it as normal and declare act or wait in order. A waiting driver can trigger on "he commits to a turn" and pre-empt with a Coordination roll modified by Initiative QR. Initiative QR7 means panic (all-out flight or all-out ramming); QR10 means you lose the round.
2. **Chase roll.** Both sides roll SC = Score × EF.
   - **Ability:** Coordination on foot; Driving, Piloting or Riding (BEF 5) in or on a vehicle or mount; Tracking against Stealth across country.
   - **Terrain:** the Challenge table applies to both sides, from open road +1 through traffic −1 and a crowded market −3 to rooftops in the rain −5.
   - **Speed edge:** +1 per class of speed advantage, up to +3, on open ground. On tight ground the same bonus comes from handling instead.
   - **Vehicle quality:** a tuned or botched vehicle carries its PM (4 − QR), from +3 to −3.
   - **Wounds:** −(level).
   - **Attention ladder:** driving alone +1; driving and shooting −1/−2; a navigator or gunner sheds a task.
3. **Compare QRs.** The better QR shifts the Lead its way by the difference in QR numbers. QR2 against QR4 is 2 points; QR3 against QR7 is 4. Equal QRs mean no change.
4. **Push.** Before rolling, either side may accept −1 to −3 for a stunt (the alley, the jump, the wrong-way lane). If they win, they add that number to the shift. If they lose, they take what the dice give.
5. **Failure.** QR7 means lost control or off-balance, at −1 next round until recovered. QR10 means a crash or fall and you are out of the chase; damage is Yield × DC ÷ 2 using a Random QR, with DC set by speed.
6. **Ending.**
   - At Lead 0 the pursuer may ram, tackle or box in, as an ordinary opposed attack.
   - At Lead 10 the quarry is out of sight, and the chase becomes Stealth against Perception or Search.
   - If the pursuer loses that, it becomes a cross-country pursuit against the standing result, at −(5 − QR).

*Example: Driving 15, BEF 5, traffic −1, driving only +1, pushing −2 gives EF 3 and SC 45. A roll of 10 is QR2 against the quarry's QR4, so the shift is 2 + 2 = 4 Lead.*

## Confident or guessing

**Confident, because the pages say so:**
- Pursuit runs on longer rounds with the same act-or-wait and pre-empt structure.
- Driving, shooting and reading the road split on the attention ladder, and a crew sheds tasks.
- Gained ground is a QR reading.
- The ground sets the EF.
- A voluntary penalty buys the stunt outright ("pay once").
- QR7 is loss of balance and QR10 is going down or ending that line of action.
- Equal QRs hold the tension rather than deciding anything.
- A secondary action cannot pre-empt.

**Guessing:**
- The Lead track, its 0–10 size and the starting values.
- Shift equal to the QR difference.
- Push adding its own size to the shift.
- The speed edge and its +3 cap.
- Round lengths.
- Every ability name except Coordination, and the BEFs.
- Crash damage.
- The −1 recovery penalty.

## Where the core rules point two ways

1. **Oppose or Yield.** "A chase's gained ground" is a QR-column reading and "ground covered" is Yield, which implies each side covers Yield × top speed. But chases are plainly opposed, where the better QR wins and ties cancel. Under Yield, a faster car gains on equal QRs; under Oppose, it does not. I chose Oppose.
2. **What a failure covers.** "Failures yield like a QR4" would give 25% ground, but that is stated for hidden results. The movement table gives ground only to QR1–4 and says nothing about distance on QR7.
3. **The ladder's size.** The table ranks two things at −1/−2, but the text says moving and shooting is "−1 each" and four shots are "−3 apiece". A secondary costs "−2 a beat". So drive-and-shoot could be −1/−1, −1/−2 or worse.
4. **Opposing a standing result.** One page says take (5 − QR) as a penalty; the other says use the QR as the base ease factor or compare rolls. This decides how losing a tail works.
5. **Build or PM.** A navigator's route could be +(5 − QR), which is never negative, or 4 − QR, where a botch gives −3.
6. **Yield or Time.** A race to a fixed point ("before the bridge goes up") reads as Time; an open chase reads as Yield.
7. **Coordination as the foot-chase ability.** The pages make movement a Coordination task, but that rule prices footing, not speed. The pages do not say what makes one runner faster than another.
8. **Tie-break.** "Equal QRs cancel" sits beside "if someone must act now, the higher Score edges it". The pages do not say whether a chase round counts as "must".
