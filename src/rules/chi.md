# Ch'i
<!--{ "parent": "magic", "order": 90 }-->

*One-pager. Ch'i is the essence of being and life. Every living thing has some; a few have a great deal, and fewer still can consciously direct the Ch'i within and around them. Where magic is a careful, generative craft, Ch'i is fast, physical and cinematic — the wire-work fantasy of **Big Trouble in Little China** and the Hong Kong films of the eighties and nineties.*

> **Ch'i replaces magic; it does not join it.** A setting can have one or the other, and a character operates in one framework, never both. Ch'i is so useful that it dominates any setting featuring it — that's by design, not an oversight, so a GM turning it on should mean it. (Its low cost is likewise deliberate: price it higher and players simply cripple themselves to buy it anyway.)

## The skill and the two paths

Ch'i is a single **skill**, and every practitioner picks a **path** — a source of inner strength — when they take it. The choice is permanent.

| Path | Formula | Recovers at |
|---|---|---|
| **Bright** (Yin) | (ST + IN) / 2 | sunrise |
| **Reflective** (Yang) | (PC + WP) / 2 | moonrise |

**BEF 4 · Limit 1** (2 if talented). Neither path is better; they differ in what comes easily, especially in controlled use — a Bright adept strikes at a distance, a Reflective one turns a blow aside for someone else.

> **Ported from 2004:** the Reflective path was *(EM + WP)/2*. EM folded into PC in this edition, so it reads (PC + WP)/2 — see [Creating Characters](/creating-characters/).

## Spending Ch'i is exhaustion, not a pool

The original tracked a reserve of points equal to three times your Ch'i score. This edition doesn't track pools: **Ch'i is an [Exhaustion](/exhaustion/) track**, exactly as mana is. Effort meters itself.

- Reaching for Ch'i is a **roll**, and the size of what you're attempting is a **modifier on that roll**, not a separate accounting step.
- Push hard and you take an exhaustion level; push hard when you're already spent and it costs more. Levels penalise Ch'i work fully and everything else at half.
- The path sets your **recovery rhythm**: Bright recovers at sunrise, Reflective at moonrise. It's pure flavour that costs nothing mechanically, and it's worth keeping — a Reflective adept husbanding their strength through a long day is a character beat.

## Three ways to use it

**1 · Unconscious — you don't die easily.** Ch'i keeps its bearer alive without being asked.

- **Wounded:** when a blow raises your wound level, roll Ch'i. On QR3 or better you recover that level as soon as you can rest for a few seconds. You can never profit from being wounded — at best you break even.
- **Dying:** when a blow would kill or incapacitate you, roll Ch'i at EF 4 for a **respite** — QR1 five minutes, QR2 one minute, QR3 thirty seconds, QR4 fifteen. During it you ignore (7 − QR) wound levels. Either way you get to whisper last words and crawl. One respite only; there is no second reprieve when it ends.
- **Second chance:** a failed roll the GM judges vital may be attempted again, the QR of the Ch'i roll setting the bonus. A failed Ch'i roll never grants one, and neither does a failed second chance — the game has to move.

**2 · Focussed — Ch'i through a skill.** For each level of Ch'i you take, choose a skill; you may choose the same skill twice.

- **Chosen once** (*the inner way*): before you roll that skill, spend effort for **+1**, or harder effort for **+2**.
- **Chosen twice** (*the outer way*): you can attempt that skill's **extraordinary feat** — rolled on the lower of your Ch'i and the skill. Failure spends the effort anyway; a fumble goes wrong, or at least publicly wrong.

This is the layer that produces the genre: *Lightning Limbs* (a ten-second spin, attacking at +2 DC and defending at +2), *Feather Feet* (100 m in three seconds, part of it up a wall or across water), *Sky Somersaults*, *Water Bug*, the Bright path's **distant strike** and the Reflective path's **umbrella parry** — a blow intercepted for someone standing metres away.

**3 · Weaving — gather, form, project.** The spectacular use, and a deliberate echo of magic's gather-then-weave.

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Gather** | Choose an energy **E**. Concentrate for √E actions, then roll at **−√E**. This is the exhaustion-triggering roll. QR2+ and you can act freely with it held; QR3 walk; QR4 you hold it only standing still, or gather half. |
| **Form** | Shape it into an **orb** — a weaving roll at −√E. The orb is 30 × E cm across. Keep control with an unmodified roll every ten seconds, or it drifts free and just sits there. |
| **Tighten** | Optional, up to seven times; each is another roll at −√E and another action. Tightening slows the leak: untightened an orb loses a point every 30 seconds, once-tightened a minute, and so on out to fifteen. |
| **Project** | A throw action at −√E. Success sends it; ordinary failure just means you still have it. |

**Gathered energy is dangerous.** Anyone — or anything unaccounted for — touching you mid-gather takes a discharge equal to what you've gathered so far. Lose control of unformed energy and you can try to disperse it safely; fail, and half goes into you and half into whatever's handy. The visuals are the point: coils of lightning crawling over your hands and feet, creeping to the torso and head when control slips, and gathering about the eyes, nose, mouth and ears when it's gone.

**Path affinity:** forming a structure your path favours is **+2**, and projecting or dispersing it **+1**.

## Extraordinary feats

*The **outer way** — what a skill taken twice unlocks. Roll on the lower of your Ch'i and the skill; failure spends the effort anyway, and a fumble goes wrong in public. **Through** is the modern skill or attribute the feat hangs off: several 2004 skills (Acrobatics, Climbing, Throwing, Unarmed Combat) are merged into broader ones here, and Stamina and Pain Resistance aren't skills at all, so those feats sit on **EN** and **WP**.*
