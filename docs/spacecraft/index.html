Spacecraft

Spacecraft are just vehicles — but the drive you give them is one of the biggest decisions a science-fiction setting makes, so they get their own page and a set of sample fleets. Pick a setting's row of the table below and you've picked how its universe feels.

The drive decides everything downstream — how long a journey takes, whether space combat is dogfighting or gunnery, whether the campaign map is a cluster of stars or a single solar system:

Setting Sublight FTL Feels like
Space Opera flies like an aeroplane instant hyperdrive jump swashbuckling; space is an ocean
Warp fractional-c impulse superluminal cruise (or fixed gates) exploration; travel time is a dial
Newtonian constant-thrust fusion torch none, or a fixed mass-shunt / magic gate hard SF; vectors and reaction mass (ForeSight's own model — and The Expanse's)
Culture trivially near-c near-instant, grid-powered post-scarcity; the ship is the biggest character

Newtonian is ForeSight's native setting: you burn toward your destination, flip at the midpoint, and burn to stop — there is no free lunch and no FTL unless a mass shunt (or, in a fantasy-SF game, a magic gate) links fixed points. The other three are here so you can lift a familiar feel off the shelf. Big hulls use the Damage Scale; a capital ship is a setpiece, not a stat line you dogfight.
