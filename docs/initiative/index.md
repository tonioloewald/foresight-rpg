# Initiative
<!--{ "parent": "conflict", "order": 1 }-->

*One-pager. Initiative answers **when do you act — and who knows what, when?** It cuts a tense situation into brief rounds and orders who moves within one. It runs combat, but equally a quick-draw, an ambushed negotiation, a courtroom, a car chase. Its companion is [Intense Action](/intense-action/): Initiative sets the **when**, Intense Action prices the **how much**.*

Tense situations run in **rounds** — brief bursts separated by waiting for someone to make a move. A round is as long as the contest's tempo demands: direct combat is roughly 3–5 seconds with random lulls (ducked behind cover, catching your breath); a pursuit or a negotiation runs longer. Don't track the clock — the GM sets the tempo to fit the scene.

## Awareness: from narrative to "shit going down"

The hard, real moment is the transition out of story-telling into a fight. Every character enters it somewhere on a spectrum:

> **Surprised** (woken by the attack) → **Startled** → **Alert** → **Ready** → **Lying in wait** (the ambusher)

**Awareness and position drive Initiative.** Coarse, obvious effects modify it: a visored helmet or a blind corner is a disadvantage; a sniper studying the scene in safety, or the enemy behind you with the height, is a large advantage. Ballpark each from about **+4** (you see everything and they don't; you're behind them) to **−4** (the reverse). Once the fight is live it tends to level out to one question — *can you see what's happening?*

### Who gets the drop: one opposed roll per side

Where each side *starts* on that spectrum is not a stat — it's a **contest**, and an asymmetric one: your watchfulness against their quietness, and theirs against yours. Each side makes two rolls:

- **Search** on the **best** Score in the group — you are as watchful as your sharpest member.
- **Stealth** on the **worst** — and as quiet as your clumsiest. *A side making no attempt at concealment doesn't roll: take **QR7**.*

Add the two QRs for an **encounter total** (low is good, like every QR), then modify it:

| Mod | The side is… |
|:--:|:--|
| **−4** | **lying in wait** — a prepared position, a planned ambush |
| **−2** | **moving cautiously** — half speed, checking corners, one scout out |
| **—** | just travelling |

Compare the totals. For the side that is **ahead**, by:

| Margin | What happens |
|:--:|:--|
| **4+** | They're **Surprised** — no declaration on round one (and if you were lying in wait, they walked right into it) |
| **3** | They're **Startled**: your choice of spring it at **observation** range, *or* take the Initiative at **contact** range |
| **2** | You're **Ready**, they're **Alert**: choose the range *or* take the Initiative and leave the range to them — not both |
| **0–1** | Both **Alert**, nobody has the drop. Range is random: **25%** contact, **75%** observation |

*Actively searching is easier than happening to notice — that's an **EF** difference on the Search roll, not a separate skill. A lookout who has been told what to look for gets the better ease factor.*

### How far away: the terrain decides, not the dice

Encounter range is a property of the **ground**, which is why it's a lookup and not a roll. Read the **Terrain Value** off the [terrain grid](/vehicles/) — contour (flat/uneven/broken) × feature (barren → heavy vegetation, marsh, ice) — the same number that divides vehicle speed:

| Terrain Value | Contact | Observation |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 0 — flat, barren | 50 m | 2 km |
| 1 | 40 m | 1 km |
| 2 | 30 m | 500 m |
| 3 | 25 m | 250 m |
| 4 | 20 m | 125 m |
| 5 | 15 m | 80 m |
| 6+ — broken, heavy vegetation | 10 m | 30 m |

**Open ground buys you two kilometres of warning; jungle buys you thirty metres.** That's the whole reason terrain matters before anyone rolls anything, and it's why a competent ambush looks for closure rather than cover.

*Designer's Note: the line this replaced read "awareness is about ½ (Perception + Stealth)" — which averaged an attribute with a skill, and averaged your **own** stealth into how well you notice **other people**. Both halves were wrong, and the second was wrong in an interesting way: it treated awareness as something you have rather than something you win. The procedure above is **ForeSight 1986 §3.10** (best-Search, worst-Stealth, sum the QRs, −4 for an ambush, −2 for caution) mapped onto this page's spectrum, with 1986's Scan/Search split collapsed into an ease factor. The range table is 1986's unchanged, except that it printed its last row as "8+" and left Terrain Value 6 — uneven heavy vegetation, i.e. ordinary forest on a slope — undefined; it reads as the bottom row.*

## The round

1. **Roll Initiative** (awareness/position-driven). Order everyone best to worst; ties broken by Score.
2. **Declare, in order, act or wait.** Acting resolves when declared. Waiting holds for a trigger.
3. **Waiting persists.** Anyone still waiting from last round stays waiting until their declaration comes round again. *The totally unaware get no declaration on round one at all — which is why ambushes are murder.*

A **QR7** Initiative roll is **panic**: act all-out offensive *or* all-out defensive (go berserk, or run and hide) — no holding back. A **QR10** is a **freeze** — you lose the three seconds. It's only three seconds; they may be your last.

## Reaction & pre-emption

When a waiting character reacts to something (usually someone else acting), they may go **immediately after** it — or try to **pre-empt** and act **just before**:

> **Pre-emption = a Coordination roll, modified by your Initiative QR** (ties: Initiative Score). Reflexes do the work; awareness only biases the odds — no good having lightning reflexes if you're confused. Succeed → just before; fail → just after. It's still a roll, so even a superb, well-placed shooter keeps real uncertainty.

A pre-emption can itself be pre-empted (the counter-sniper), but you'll rarely go deeper. And **you never see each other's Initiative** — that uncertainty is the whole point.

> **You never know for certain you'll act first.** Pretty sure, never certain. The gunslinger *probably* draws first; the sniper *probably* fires before the hostage-taker reacts; neither is guaranteed. It's exactly why a police sniper in a standoff is loath to just shoot a person holding a gun on a hostage — and why these scenes work here and collapse into "highest number wins" everywhere else.

*E.g. an archer waits — trigger: "the mage does something." The mage starts a spell; the archer pre-empts (QR of Initiative). On success the arrow lands before the spell goes off — but a confident archer can still be a hair too slow.*

## Beyond combat

This is **initiative for any tense situation** where timing and nerve decide the outcome, not only when weapons come out:

- **The quick-draw / Iai duel** — both waiting, each triggered by the other's move; pre-emption *is* the duel.
- **Business machinations** — who commits first, who holds a move in reserve, who read the room (awareness) before anyone spoke.
- **A legal battle** — objecting *just* before the answer lands; holding an argument for its moment.
- **A car chase** — see [Pursuit](/pursuit/): longer rounds, the same act-or-wait and pre-empt.

*Designer's Note: the experiments that drove this whole system were the Western shootout, the same shootout with a dastardly sniper, the Iai duel, and the hostage standoff. None of them resolve correctly in any other TTRPG — the nastiest edge cases of combat, which vastly more complex systems (Aftermath, I'm looking at you) never even tried to model. They cost one page here because the actor never *knows* they'll go first: every ruling is "make an Initiative roll and read its QR." Nothing to memorize, and the probability arrows all point the right way.*
