[3.0] Resolution of Character Actions

Designer's Notes: Various procedures and game situations will call for tasks to be resolved, ·rolls· to be made, and so on. The Resolution system in ForeSight is central to the game system, and should be clearly understood by the GM, and preferably the players as well. Definition: In ForeSight a given task will have two associated numbers. One of these is the relevant pes, or Primary Chance of Success, which is the PCS of the skill, or the value of the attribute, the GM feels is most applicable to the task; the other is the net EF or Ease Factor, which is that of the skill chosen 'by the GM. The Ease Factor may be modified by circumstances, ie. the sum of any relevant, and any GM decreed, modifiers. If the task involves a skill, then the EF - the skill's BEF plus these modifiers. (Note: a character's BEF for some skill may be higher (or lower) than that listed owing to some talent or aptitude helshe possesses.) If the task involves an attribute (eg. ST to shift some massive object), then the value of that attribute is used as the PCS, and the BEF is treated as 7, but the system otherwise functions as for skills.

Notes on Ease Factors til An Ease Factor adjusted above ten (10) is treated as ten. This is done after adding all relevant modifiers (eg. a task with BEF 6, and a +S and a -2 modifier would end up as EF 9, not EF 8). [ii] An Ease Factor adjusted to 0 is treated as one half (O.S); -1 is treated as one quarter (0.2S) ; and an Ease Factor lower than this is treated as zero (0). So the possible Ease Factors are:

Determining the Result of a Task Procedure: A task involving a skill (or attribute) is resolved as follows: determine the net EF and multiply it by the PCS, and call the result the SC (success chance). Roll D100, and call the result D, and compare it to the SC: if D s SC then the result depends upon the ratio DISC: if DISC s 0.1, then the result is called a Ouality Rating One (abbreviated OR 1); if 0.1 < DISC S 0.2, then the result is a OR, 2; if 0.2 < DISC S O.S, then the result is a OR 3; and if O.S < DISC S 1, then the result is a OR 4. Exception: a 99 is never better (lower) than a OR 4, and a 100 is never better than a OR 7. If D > SC then the result is a OR 7 unless D is a multiple of ten, in which case the result is a OR 10. A summary of results is given below.

foreSight [3.0] Resolution Procedures OR 1: rolled less than one tenth of success chance, a brilliant success: 100% of information or substance is yielded, if that was objective of task; the task took SO% of the expected time to complete, if speed was desired, and so forth. OR 2: rolled less than one fifth of success chance, a very good success: 90% of information or substance is yielded, if that was objective of task; the task took 7S% of the expected time to complete, if speed was desired, and so forth. OR 3: rolled less than half of success chance, a good success: 7S% of information or substance is yielded, if that was objective of task; the task took the expected time to complete, and so forth. OR 4: rolled less than success chance, a bare success: SO% of information or substance is yielded, if that was objective of task; the task took twice as long to complete as expected, and so forth. OR 7: rolled over success chance, a failure : perhaps 2S% of information or substance is yielded, and some false or extraneous matter also, if that was objective of task; the task took twice as long to complete as expected, and so forth. OR 10: rolled over success chance, a botch-up: no useful information or substance is yielded, although extraneous material might be mistaken for that desired, if that was the objective of the task; the task also took twice as long to complete as expected.

Example: a character attempts a task with a skill of PCS - 1S, with a BEF of 3 + G. The planet the task is being performed on has heavy (HY) gravity, and therefore the character substitutes hislher HY familiarity of 3 into the BEF formula. The GM rules that the task is not routine, and assigns a modifier of -1 to it. The EF of the task is therefore S (ie. 3 + G (-3) -1). The SC is therefore 7S (S x 1S). If the player rolls 1 to 7, hislher character will achieve a OR 1; 8 to 15 will achieve a OR 2; 16 to 37 will achieve a OR 3; 38 to 75 will achieve a OR 4; a roll above 7S, but not a multiple of ten, will result in a OR 7 (a failure); and a OR 10 will result on a roll of 80, 90, or 100 (a botch-up).

Using A Skill with a Field Some skills are simply methods of handling knowledge (especially Implement, Recall, Diagnose, and Repair), and can only be used in conjunction with a relevant field. Implementation and recall are used with theoretical fields and humanities, while diagnosis and repair are used with technical fields. Other rules allow fields for Dancing, Unarmed Combat, Search, Scan, and so forth. Sometimes a skill will only be applicable to a task if the character possessing the skill also possesses a field, ego a character with Scan or Search would also need Biology to determine the characteristics of some organism, and to patch up a wounded friend not only diagnose and repair skills, but the fields emergency and/or standard medical techniques, would be necessary. Such a task is designated a skllllfleid task. If the character has the requisite knowledge, then the task is resolved with one of the modifications to EF being the familiarity modifier the character has associated with the field he/she is using. Eg. a character who has just learnt emergency medical techniques might have associated with it a familiarity modifier of -2, and therefore both his/her diagnosis and re.pair tasks incur a -2 modifier.

[3.3] Seduction Seduction is performed in four stages. It may only be attempted against an NPC who is sexually compatible with the attempting PC, or it will almost automatically fail (-4, or worse, at all stages). If the PC is not actually compatible with his/her target the she/he must make a WP roll before each stage to go through with it. H an S result has already been achieved against the NPC during interaction by that PC (as spokesperson) the the attempt may begin at stage [iii], if the S was achieved by someone else in the group, then the attempt may begin at stage [ii]. [i] The seduction attempt begins with an opening: a witty line, a glance, whatever, and is performed at +2 (using Seduction skill, of course). The NPC then makes an Empathy roll at EF = OR (of Seduction roll), and figures out what is going on if he/she achieves OR 3 or better. [ii] Next the PC attempts to briefly impress himlher self upon his/her subject, by demonstrating his/her Wit, charm, knowledge, and taste. This is resolved using Seduction (no modifier). [iii] Next the PC attempts publicly acceptable intimacies (eg. kissing, and so forth, in 20th century Western Society) upon hislher subject; this is resolved by a normal Seduction roll.

[3.4] Repair There are two repair procedures in ForeSight, these are termed cursory and diagnostic, and refer to the methods used to determine the nature of the problem. Note that medical techniques (especially removing combat derived damage) are simply special cases of these. Cursory Repair

Procedure: the repairing character must have the appropriate field of knowledge, and tools. The character makes a Search roll in order to determine the location of the problem. This takes a base time equal to the base time (for the object's size) multiplied by the coefficients for complexity and condition of the object, in five minute periods. A OR 3 or better is treated as a success, and if achieved, the required replacement parts must be procured for repair to proceed. The object is repaired by attempting a Repair roll at (OR (of Search roll) + 1); ego at -3 if the Search was resolved to be OR 2. The EF of the roll may be further modified by the condition of the object. The repair time is calculated as for diagnosis, but in hour increments. A OR 7 indicates that no repair was achieved (but the PC still knows what's wrong), while OR 10 indicates that the PC has caused more damage.

Designer's Notes: sometimes the problem is obvious, and no diagnosis is necessary, in which case repair may immediately be performed at -2; ego a person suffering a gunshot wound, or a car with a flat tyre. Note also that Emergency Medical Techniques may only be used this way. Diagnostic Repair

Procedure: the character must have the appropriate knowledge, and tools. The character attempts to diagnose the nature of the fault, that is makes a Diagnose roll at the appropriate modifier for object condition. The base time for the task is that given by the object's size, multiplied by coefficients for the complexity and condition of the object, in increments of fifteen minutes. If successful, then the replacement parts necessary must be procured for the repair to be completed. Note that the normal condition for

[3.6] Research In Foresight I divide Research into two types: that which attempts to adapt existing knowledge or theory to some use the researcher has in mind for it, and that which involves attempting to extend a field of knowledge so that it encompasses some new observation. These are termed Applied Research and Theoretical Research, respectively. An example of Applied Research would be our current attempts to design Fusion Power Plants using the models we have of the atom, and the strong and weak nuclear forces we have the theory (we hope) we just haven't been able to implement it yet; an example of Theoretical Research would be the attempt to figure out how White Dwarfs can be fitted into the formulae which seem to explain other stars (which seems to have been done).

Applied Research Designer's Notes: this is where I start tossing around the term GM's discretion, I guess. The character wants to adapt some current theory to implementation with current "technology": the GM must decide what the appropriate fields of knowledge are, and whether the task is possible, marginal (the theory mayor may not be sound), or impoSSible. The character must first do some Research (using the Recall skill); success will inform the character whether the desired implementation is possible, marginal, or impossible, with current technology. H it is possible (or marginal, if the character wishes to continue), that the implementation will work, then the character attempts to so Implement the theory, at -OR (of research). If this yields OR 3 or better, then the program has been successful, and the same, or another, character may proceed with a design, with an additional adjustment of -OR (of implementation) to the diagnose roll. How much time all this takes is up to the GM, but could be several years, for a complex or innovative device.

Theoretical Research Designer's Notes: there are two sorts of ways this is likely to crop up in play. The character may be a researcher of some sort attempting, for whatever reason, to further and expand his/her field, gain a Nobel Prize, or have a famous Paradigm named after himlher, or the character may be in the considerably more likely position of wanting to explain some observation in terms of the theory he/she knows (on the spot, with minimal references). To do this the character will require the relevant fields. If the observation has to be made (ie. data must be gathered), then the character makes a Search roll. Once the data is gathered, the character must Recall the relevant knowledge (from memory and convenient references, lack of these makes this task difficult), at -OR (of Search; if data gathered by third party, then add one to the Search OR they achieved). Finally the analysis of the observation is made by making an Implement roll at -OR (of Recall), the OR of which determines the quality of the analysis. A OR 1 result may, at the GM's option, be of some interest, and worthy of a write-up in Nature, or some such journal.

Procedure: Both the sides involved make a Scan roll (using the best SC - EFxPCS in the group) and a Stealth roll (using the worst) and add the QRs together (this sum is reduced by four if the party was attempting an ambush, by two if the characters were proceeding cautiously, ie. at half speed, and is unadjusted otherwise). If one side's sum is four or more lower than any other side's then the former is said to have surprised the latter (if an ambush was attempted, then the latter walked right into it); if one side's total was three lower than the other's, that side may surprise the other at observation range, or start with the initiative at contact range; if one side's total was two lower, it may start combat at either contact or observation range (its choice) and determine initiative normally, or it may have the initiative and leave the choice of range to the other side; otherwise, the initial range is determined randomly (25% chance of contact range, 75% chance of observation range). [3.11] Teamwork Rather than provide hard and fast rules for teamwork in ForeSight, I'd like to give some common-sense approaches to the topic, each reflecting the different ways in which teamwork can improve the likelihood of tasks being performed quickly, and/or successfully.

So, at this point I will simply state that common sense is probably the best approach to such situations.

Division of Labour Perhaps the easiest form of teamwork to use involves splitting up tasks into small parts, and then resolving each task separately. Eg. if a bunch of characters want to design a new sort of laser pistol (smaller, nastier, and all that), then one team might work on a superior lasing "tube", another might work on phaseconjugate mirror design, another on a more efficient capacitor, and another on the targeting mechanism. Each team might consist of four people (and a bunch of computers, and stuff). Two are engineers who perform "design" tasks, and come up with alternative designs; two are technicians who assemble ("construct") and test prototypes. Finally, a project coordinator performs "diagnose" actions on the prototypes, and makes final decisions on which will be accepted, rejected, and returned for further improvement.

Many Hands Make Light Work All else being equal, two people are better at, say, lifting a "thing" than is one. The ForeSight GM will have to make spot decisions on this one. Eg. if two people try to lift something, then simply adding their strengths is not going to work (Strength is not linear in ForeSight, but polynomial; sorry about that. .. ). In this case, add their carrying capacities.

A Second Opinion If two people diagnose a patient, or ailing device, then they should be more likely to make a sound diagnosis. A character should only have to make an (EF7) IN roll to realise that a superior dianosis is, in fact, superior. Similarly, characters using Implement to form hypotheses should be given (unmodified) Implement rolls to realise that a superior, rival hypothesis is in fact superior. (they may, of course, refuse to admit it.) Characters who fail the above rolls will tend to believe in their own opinions if they are assertive, and rival opinions if they are receptive (intellectually). You might like to make EF5 Assertive rolls for the characters involved (success implying belief in one's own ideas), but I wouldn't advise it.

[3.12] Skill Guidelines The following comprise guidelines for using FS skills for a variety of tasks. These should not be seen as the way to use the skills covered, but as no more than what they are. Examples. There are three primary sorts of example given in the rules which follow. Simple resolution procedures merely require a single skill roll to be performed for a task to be completed. Opposed resolution procedures involve the execution of a task which is liable, later, to become subject to countermeasures.

Tiered resolution procedures involve the execution of a task which builds upon the results of a previous task. Opposed and tiered resolutions will generally involve more than one roll.

Designing a Task When a character decides to do something not explicitly covered by the rules, it is the GM's lot to determine the task's parameters. First, the GM must decide which skill or attribute is being used (adventurous GMs may wish to use combinations, or averages, of a number of PCSs/attributes); second, the difficulty, and consequent EF modifier, of the task must be decided; filially, the GM may determine the task to be opposed or tiered, as per some of the following examples, rather then simple.

Examples Acrobatics. Simple: a character who wishes to perform an unusually difficult physical feat may be required by the GM to make an Acrobatics roll at some modifier. Acrobatics is an inherently difficult skill, designed for inherently difficult tasks. As such, a task should have to be very difficult in order to incur a large negative modifier. Artistic Composition. Simple: this skill can be used to layout and conceive artwork, and to reach sensible conclusions about the quality and intentions of existing artwork. Artistic Depiction. Simple: this skill can be used to draft or otherwise create images which will look like what the artist has in mind. In general, it can be used to create uninspiring works in an established format (eg. charcoal drawings of tourists).

Tiered: this skill can be used to turn an idea the artist has (ie. a previously performed Composition) into a finished work. The OR of the artwork is the higher [worse] of its composition and depiction rolls. An artist may make many attempts to depict the same composition. The GM may apply negative modifiers based on a composition's complexity. Climbing. Simple: a character can use Climbing to scale a steep incline, or remain in place without falling on an inclined surface. The GM should modify Climbing tasks on the basis of equipment used, lighting conditions, and the surface climbed on. The results can be interpreted, as always, in terms of what the PC is attempting to achieve: speed, safety, stealth, or whatever. A character attempting to climb stealthily would use the worse of hislher Stealth and Climbing PCSs and BEFs in concert. Rugged, huh? Confidence. Opposed: this skill is used to convince people of things, often using a mixture of half-truths, emotional manipulation, and innuendo. The GM should modify the roll depending on the subject's attitude towards the character using Confidence. Finally, the subject should make an EF=OR Empathy roll, and if successful (Empathy fails on a QR4), will realise that he/she is being manipulated.

Dancing. Simple: this skill is used when a character wishes to dance. If the dance involves a group, then the average OR determines how impressive the group is overall. (A trained eye will spot who is screwing up.) Disguise. Opposed: this skill is used when a character is attempting to look like some other person (or whatever). Eg. an actor taking a role, or a spy taking on a cover identity. Those encountering the Disguised character, who are familiar with the character being respresented, receive an EF-OR (of Disguise) Empathy roll, and if successful (Empathy fails on QR4) realise that the person may be an imposter. Those not familiar with the character being represented receive a similar roll, but only detect the Disguise on a QR1 or 2. If another skilled character does a Disguised character's makeup, then the average of their ORs determines the OR of the Disguise. If the Disguised character asks the assistance of other characters, and these characters are able to penetrate the Disguise and give advice (note that this will be easier for these characters if they are familiar with the subject of the Disguise), then the OR of the Disguise is reduced [improved] by one. Empathy. Simple: this skill can be used to determine the demeanors of people in a character's vicinity. One roll should be made per "group". Note that Empathy fails on a QR4. EVA. Whenever a character performs an AG related task while wearing protective attire, he/she should use the lower of hislher appropriate and EVA PCSs. (Eg. a character performing a Climbing task in protective attire uses the lower of his/her Climbing and EVA PCSs, together with his/her Climbing BEF.) Gambling. Opposed: In a multi-player gambling game of skill, each player should place a stake and make a Gambling roll, the best OR winning (tied players rerolling). Where each player plays against the dealer, the dealer should receive a + 1 modifier. A character who cheats receives a +N modifier, and each player receives an EF'"'N Scan (or Search if deliberately looking for signs of cheating) roll to detect it. If the house is cheating, the EF of the roll is reduced by two.

LIterary Composition. Simple: this skill can be used to outline/plan out a literary work, or to assess the quality and intention of an existing work. Literary Execution. Simple: this skill can be used to create a prose work in an established format (eg. a lab report, log/diary entry, or tract of doggerel). Tiered: this skill is used to turn an outline and disorganised material, assembled via Literary Composition, into a finished work. The OR of the finished work is equal to the average of the composition and execution rolls'. The GM may wish to modify the EF of the Execution based on the complexity of the composition.

Musical Composition. Simple: this skill can be used to compose a piece of music, or to assess the quality and intention of an existing piece. A piece can only really be finished when the composer has arranged it (see below). Musical Performance. Simple: this skill can be used by a character to playa piece of music on an instrument whose corresponding field the character has learnt. The GM may modify the task's EF in the case of a difficult piece. The OR of the performance can never be better than the OR of the piece. Tiered: this skill can be used to arrange a piece of music (for an orchestra or band). The OR of the piece is equal to the average of those of its composition and arrangement. Of course, a complex piece will be more difficult to arrange. Together with Composition, Performance can be used to perform improvised pieces, the OR of the improvisation being the poorest of the Performance and Composition rolls. Navigation. Simple: this skill is used to determine the path a character will take to his/her desired destination. The yield OR can be assumed to represent how efficient the path will be (so a QR1, giving 100%, would be a direct path; while a QR3, giving 75%, would be an inefficient path, requiring a third again the distance). Pickpocket. Opposed: this skill is used to remove other people's possessions from their pockets, belts, holsters, etc.. If successful, then some item or other is removed from the subject's person, but the subject receives an EF=OR (of Pickpocket) Scan (Search if actively looking for pickpockets) roll, and if successful notices the thief.

Scan. Simple: if a character is wandering about aimlessly, and approaches something which he/she would probably want to know about, but isn't actively searching for, then he/she should make a Scan roll (possibly modified for the inherent difficulty of seeing whatever it is, which can be represented by the size and cover modifiers for fire combat in [4.0]). Note that Scan fails on a QR4. Search. Simple: this skill is used when a character is searching for something. The GM should modify any roll for the time spent looking, and the difficulty of finding the object. Search can also be used to hide an object, to choose a good hiding place, or to spot a vantage point overlooking a searching character's current location. Stealth. Opposed: this skill is used by a character wishing to avoid attention. There is another way of handling this (apart from that given in [2.2]), which you may prefer. A sneaking or hiding character makes a Stealth roll. Anyone who might otherwise spot the character must get a superior OR with Scan (or an equal/superior OR with Search if actively searching) to notice him/her. The EF of a Stealth attempt should be modified for the suitability of the terrain, and availability of cover.
