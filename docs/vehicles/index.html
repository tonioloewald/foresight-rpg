Vehicles

A vehicle is an object you travel in. Ground, water, and air share one stat block; spacecraft are vehicles too, split onto their own page because their drives define whole settings.

Cruise is the comfortable all-day speed; Max is the redline. Terrain says where those speeds hold — a road car divides its speed on rough ground, an off-roader barely notices. Passengers and Cargo are capacity. As with every object, the interesting variety is adjectives: sporty, luxury, police, cheap/rusty — see the Vehicle Modification set on the Equipment page. Travel time, pursuit, and crashes run off these numbers rather than a separate subsystem.

The terrain grid

Terrain is two questions, not a list: what shape is the ground, and what's growing on it? Cross them and you get one number — the Terrain Value — and that number is all any rule needs.

Contour ↓ / Feature → Barren Light veg. Medium veg. Heavy veg. Marsh Ice
Flat 1 1 2 4 2 2
Uneven 1 2 3 6 3 3
Broken 3 4 5 8 — 5

Settled ground extends the features rather than replacing the grid: suburban 0 (1 where there's real cover — walls, hedges, parked ranks), urban 0 (2 once it's genuinely built up). A city is fast and blind, which is a different thing from rough.

Three rules read this one number:

  • Speed. Cruise and Max are divided by TV ÷ 2 (never less than 1); Pursuit and Maneuver modifiers drop by the full TV; Redline rises by TV ÷ 2 — rough ground breaks vehicles. A vehicle type also has a Limit: the highest TV it can enter at all, and its own adjustment to the value (an off-roader shrugs; a sports sedan doesn't).
  • Encounter distance. See Initiative — TV 0 gives two kilometres of warning, TV 6+ gives thirty metres.
  • Planet generation. The world generator assigns every province a contour and a feature, so a generated world arrives pre-stocked with the numbers travel and encounters need. One grid, filled in at the planetary scale, read at the tactical one.

Designer's Note: the grid is the part of 1986's terrain treatment worth keeping; what made it cumbersome was the other part — characters were expected to carry a familiarity per grid square, so the matrix quietly became a bookkeeping tax on the character sheet. That's gone, under the same decision that deleted the gravity/environment/temperature familiarity tables: terrain familiarity is a flagship example of long-term familiarity ("knows this jungle"), never a subsystem. The grid stays because it's doing real work for three different rules and costs one table to learn.
