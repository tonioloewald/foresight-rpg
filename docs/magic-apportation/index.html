Apportation

move objects at a distance; magical transport

A Fundamental — a Field of Magical Knowledge whose applications reach 50m and last 10min; the usual props are a pole, or a glove. Learn it Basic (1 yr) for the easy end, Advanced (3 yr) for the deep.

Teleportation

Intensity
5
Code
§3A9
Source
apportation

Cause an object massing no more than 100kg to be transferred instantly from one place to another, no further away than 100km. (A QR10 indicates permanent loss/death/annihilation of the object so transported, in addition to normal backfire.) (Immediate.) !!! This assumes the invoker has considerable familiarity with the destination, or knowledge of its exact coordinates (in some useful form). - If the invoker is completely unfamiliar with the destination, then intensity increases by two. - If the invoker is vaguely familiar with, or has a description of, the location, intensity increases by one. In either of these two cases, loss/death/annihilation occurs if a QR7 or 10 is achieved.

Create Portal

Intensity
7
Code
§3A13
Source
apportation

Create a "portal": a passage between a 3m×2m "doorway" within the invoker's range and another such "doorway" in some other place (chosen with as much accuracy as the invoker can muster) on this or another plane of existence. A creature entering one of the two "doorways" is instantly transported to the other. Otherwise, the same notes apply to this application as apply to Teleportal (§3A11). Note that this application is not bestowed, so if used on its own, the portal collapses when its invoker passes through (assuming that the other end is out of range). !!! Note: in both portal effects, the "doorways" appear as rectangles of blackness, with occasional flickers of colour. Rays of light, sounds, and inanimate objects (unless carried or pushed by a creature) do not pass through. Optional Teleport & Gate Rules. Applications of Apportation marked with an asterisk (*) receive the following modifications to their intensity: - Do not change the intensity if the origin is extremely similar in both form and function to the destination. (Eg. teleporting from one plush throne-room to another throne room of similar appearance; or opening a gate between two stone doorways of similar size and construction.) - Add one to the intensity if the origin is similar in either shape or form to the destination. (Eg. teleporting to a throne room from a barn decorated so as to resemble it; or opening a gate between a stone doorway leading into a corridor and another opening into an alcove.) - Add two to the intensity if the origin and destination are not radically different. (Eg. teleporting from an open field to a grassy knoll.) This also applies if the origin and destination are not very distinct locations — that is, land-marks or easily named locations. A room would be more "distinct" than a corridor, a crossroad more so than a road, a hill more so than a field. - Add three (or more) to the intensity if the origin and destination are radically different. (Eg. teleporting from the middle of a desert to the shore of a lake — you might add four to the intensity.)
