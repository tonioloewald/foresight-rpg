## Design history: planet generation (informs the ch.7 rewrite)
- **The published population loop is a survivor, not the original design.** Star-system generation originally produced an *iterative broad-strokes post-settlement history* for a planet; it proved **too tedious to actually resolve at the table**, and was streamlined down to the colonisation/population loop that shipped in [7.2] (walk the date forward in 25-year steps, roll against `(120 − 24×HI) × Density`, then settler type → initial PD → current population). Tonio, 2026-08.
- **Implication for the rewrite: bring it back — as code.** The constraint that killed it was **manual** resolution (a human grinding dice and bookkeeping), and that constraint is gone. Automated, an iterative history generator is cheap to run and produces exactly the texture a GM wants handed to them. This is a prime candidate for an interactive generator on the site, alongside the character builder.
- **The original anticipated this.** [7.0]'s designer's notes deliberately kept the system "dumb" — explicitly so a GM could *plug it into a computer without having to add any algorithms* — and note the whole thing, down to rough planetary maps, could be machine-generated. So automating it isn't a departure from the 1986 design; it's the thing it was shaped for and couldn't have in 1986.
- **What to keep from the pruning:** the *output* still has to be skimmable. Automate the grind, not the reading — a GM wants a paragraph of history and a few hooks, not a 40-line audit trail (though "show the working" behind a toggle is cheap and nice).

## Planned: personalized rulebook / cheat sheet from a character (2026-08)
- **The pitch:** a character sheet can emit *the rules that apply to that character* — "you're a magician, here are your magic rules". We're unusually well placed for this because the rules are already **one-pagers per concept**, i.e. atomic units you can include or exclude; most rulebooks can't do it because their chapters interleave.
- **⚠ Unify with the settings/configuration filter — don't build two.** Choosing a setting filters the corpus by axes (Tech, Magic, Ch'i, Psionics, Divine); building a character filters it *further* by what that character actually has (skills, BFs, fields, fundamentals). Same predicate evaluation, two scopes (campaign-wide, character-wide). Two separate filters will drift and disagree about whether a page belongs.
- **Mechanism:** each page declares a small inclusion predicate in its metadata (e.g. include when the setting has Magic *and* the character has Mana). Additive, page by page — not a new subsystem.
- **Two distinct outputs, don't conflate:** (a) **personalized rulebook** — every applicable one-pager in reading order, now emittable as a **per-character ePub volume** (multi-volume landed in tosijs-ui 1.9.3+); (b) **cheat sheet** — one page of this character's numbers plus only the procedures they'll roll (cf. the existing GM cheat sheet).

## Piety refined: a drawable resource, deed-driven recovery scaled by depth (2026-08)
**Tonio's refinement:** Piety is **more of a power pool managed with a skill** than a plain skill gate. You *deliberately draw on it* (unlike fatigue, which accrues as a side-effect of effort), and the **Piety skill manages the drawing**. Recovery is **graduated, exactly like wounds**: a minor deficit comes back through ordinary devotion and observance, while a **big deficit needs deeds** — the same way a light wound heals with rest and a major one doesn't.

Why this is better than "all recovery is deeds": it lets a devout character use small favours *routinely* without a quest attached, while a called-in miracle puts them in real, visible debt. Deed-driven recovery stays the flavour of the deep end, where it belongs, instead of taxing every minor blessing.

It also preserves the property that matters most: **Piety stays visible on the character sheet** (see Design Document → "don't hide a player's own character from them"), and the depth of the deficit is exactly the information a player needs to decide whether to spend now or hold.

**Two conflicts with the current prose, to resolve:**
- `exhaustion.md` says divine favour "is earned, not rested" full stop — needs the graduated version (shallow: devotion; deep: deeds).
- Its Designer's Note says "the stamina/mana/piety point-pools are all gone, and good riddance". That still holds for stamina and mana; Piety is now the exception.
- [ ] **Open fork — pool or levels?** "Power pool" implies counting points; the wound analogy implies a **level track** (0–N, recovery deepest-first, difficulty scaling with depth). The level model keeps decided-simplification #4's real benefit (a check you can't fudge beats bookkeeping people forget) while giving exactly the graduated recovery described. **Recommend levels; needs Tonio's call before the prose is rewritten.**

## Reading the 1986 source: derive, don't guess
- **The 1986 playtesters were maths / comp-sci / physics / economics people**, and the text was proofread on top of that. So the rules are **internally consistent** — which makes a mangled formula *recoverable by derivation* rather than a coin-flip. Use this when OCR wrecks a formula: reconstruct it, then check it against the physics/arithmetic and against the same formula where it appears elsewhere in the book.
  - Worked: `96√(s/a)` travel-time constant — falls out of 2√(1.5×10¹¹·s/5a).
  - Worked: "ten minutes (`SOO` seconds…)" → **600** — confirmed twice over by "ten minutes" and "200 turns of ground combat" × 3s.
  - Worked: misjump software recovery `(5+D5)/(TL−5)` minutes — **verified**, not a guess: the chapter says only **TL7+** ships can carry interstellar drives, so the denominator is always ≥2 (no singularity), recovery falls monotonically with TL (TL7 3–5 min → TL9 1.5–2.5 min), and `D5` is a defined ForeSight die (D10 halved, round up).
- Corollary: where a reconstruction *can't* be made consistent, that's a signal the reading is wrong — leave it verbatim and flag it rather than shipping a plausible-looking number.

## Decided: AP becomes a family of quirks (2026-08, Tonio)
**A linear 3–16 attribute is the wrong shape for looks.** "Attractive" isn't one axis — it's *striking*, *imposing*, *trustworthy-faced*, *forgettable*, *distinctive*, *scarred*, each helping and hurting different things. And the effect is audience-dependent: it matters whether this crowd is oriented toward you at all. A quirk family says "imposing — better at intimidation, worse at blending in"; a single number can't.

This is the **second prong** of the attribute test in action (see below): the Design Document defends AP on *independence* grounds — looks correlate with nothing else — and that defence still holds. But independence is necessary and **not sufficient**; AP also has to discriminate between characters *and* apply uniformly enough to be a scale. It fails the second. **Supersedes the AP paragraph in the Coordination note**, which should be updated to say why independence alone didn't save it.

**⚠ Consequence — three formulas lose their driver, and they may converge.** AP currently drives:

| Skill | Formula | covers |
|---|---|---|
| Charm | `(AP+PC)/2` | likeability, persuasion, seduction, leadership |
| Confidence | `(AP+IN)/2` | composure, bluffing, lying, intimidation |
| Perform | `(PC+AP)/2` | music, acting, oratory |

If AP is simply dropped, the obvious replacements make all three some blend of **PC and IN** — i.e. **three social skills with identical formulas**, differentiated only by level. That may be acceptable (they are genuinely different skills), but it's worth choosing deliberately rather than arriving at it. Options: single-attribute formulas (Charm = PC, Confidence = IN, Perform = PC), or deliberately different blends, with the AP-family quirks supplying the situational modifier on top.

|---|---|---|---|
| Charm | `(AP+PC)/2` | **`(IN+PC)/2`** | AP-family quirks; EM-flavour quirks |
| Confidence | `(AP+IN)/2` | **`IN`** | AP / EM quirks — it's *keeping your story straight* |
| Perform | `(PC+AP)/2` | **`(IN+PC)/2`** | AP / EM quirks |

Charm and Perform now **deliberately** share a formula — chosen, not drifted into; they differ by level and by what they cover. **AP drives no skill at all**, so it's free to become the quirk family. Load is now **PC 14 · CO 13 · IN 8 · ST 2 · EN 0 · WP 0 · AP 0** — IN is a genuine third pillar rather than a minor stat.

**Disguise:** not a separate skill; it was folded into *Confidence*'s coverage, which is now wrong — Confidence is `IN` alone while disguise wants `IN+PC`, i.e. **Perform**. Moved it there. Keeping it folded rather than splitting it out, because it fails the two-way test: a disguise you can't carry off simply fails, so performance competence genuinely transfers. Note 1986 split the **craft** (someone else can do your makeup — their QR averages in) from the **wearing**; that suggests the craft half is a **Field**, the wearing is Perform. Worth preserving that distinction when the Fields page is drafted.
- [ ] **Power-pool access as a quirk family, baselined by the setting axis** (Tonio, 2026-08). The Magic/Psionics/Ch'i/Divine axes already exist as ordinal levels (`none · rare-weak · rare · common-weak · common`); make the level set the **character-side baseline**, with deviation from it carrying as perk / quirk / flaw. **Norstrilia**: Psionics common → *lacking* telepathy is a flaw. **Earthsea**: Magic rare → any access is a quirk, full access a perk. Same rule as myopia-with-spectacles — a quirk is a deviation from the *local* default, and the setting supplies the default.
  - **Gain: partial access becomes expressible.** Today the Intrinsic *Magical / Mystic / Psionic / Ch'i Talent* factor is binary. Replace with a ladder: **none · a trickle · full**.
  - **Guard:** in a high-magic setting access is free (it *is* the baseline) and the variation is skill, not access. Don't charge a slot for what everyone has.
- [ ] **Power/finesse as a framework template** (Tonio, 2026-08) — ST/CO power+finesse of body, WP/IN of mind; a new power system is built by naming its oomph and control attributes. **Magic is already an instance** (Mana gathers = power; Incantation weaves = finesse). **Psionics** (vestigial 1986 material) becomes a mental framework: **WP oomph, IN precision**.
  - [ ] **Write the power-vs-precision option into the Magic one-pager** when it's drafted. Design target: a magical **fencer / knife-thrower** — high Incantation, low Mana, achieving by placement what Tim the Enchanter achieves by volume (*ignite the powder in his pistol* rather than throw a fireball). The structure already allows it; it needs saying, and the economy must not punish it.
  - **Anti-pattern being designed against:** a system whose only variable is intensity has no craft in it — saying the magic word *harder* (Harry Potter). Amplitude is the easiest dial to write and the least interesting.
  - **⚠ Balance risk, the serious one:** precision magic is potentially far more efficient, and if priced by energy expended it becomes strictly better, inverting the anti-pattern into *always find the clever tiny effect*. Apply the keystone — **price the capability, not its costume**: igniting a pistol charge is priced by killing an armed man at range, not by the joules. **Precision is knowledge-gated** (you must know the powder is there — a Field), which is the natural counterweight and gives IN a third consistent job alongside learning rate and field depth.
- [ ] **Flaws pay in capacity, not currency** (Tonio, 2026-08) — a blind character plausibly concentrated on skills blindness doesn't cripple *and had more hours for them*; that tradeoff is real and belongs in the build. **The compensation must be non-fungible**: points don't remember where they came from (which is why blindness funds telepathy elsewhere), but redirected attention can only be spent inside the space the flaw leaves open, so the exploit closes by construction. Denominate it in **time** — a field, a deeper field, or a familiarity — matching how ForeSight already prices a life. **Same trade as ageing; write them the same way.**
  - **Sizing test (Tonio, 2026-08): *would anyone take this deal on the merits?* If yes, the offset is too big.** A blind person would still, in almost every case, rather not be blind — that must stay true *after* the offset, or the offset has become a price and the flaw becomes shoppable. Mirror of the quirk-vs-perk test (*would the drawback bite?*); the answers must be **yes** and **no**.
  - **Falls out: there is no flaw point table, ever.** Nothing here is purchasable, so blindness and haemophilia never need costing against each other. A whole chapter other systems carry that this one doesn't.
  - **Settled by the test:** the capped explicit allowance (one field/familiarity, GM-approved, never routing around the flaw's own domain). One field is nowhere near tempting enough to buy blindness with — safe, and it matches the intuition.
- [ ] **State plainly in the rules that perks are limited by credibility, not balance** (Tonio, 2026-08: *"there is no game balance here"*). The ~3 cap is a rule of thumb for *a pile of perks is ridiculous*, not a balance figure. `creating-characters.md` currently gives the cap ("more and your character becomes a basket case") without the reason — add it, and say explicitly that equal points do not mean equal characters. The economy stops arithmetic nonsense; the GM and plain credibility stop fictional nonsense.
- [ ] **Perks suppress capacity, as the mirror of flaws redirecting it** (Tonio, 2026-08). Someone very good-looking may be a few skills short because problems got solved before they had to learn anything — *the traffic stopped, so they never learned to cross the road carefully.* **General form: a reliably available capability atrophies its mundane substitute** (levitation → never learned to climb; wealth → never learned to haggle) — the same lens the magic rules use for *benchmark against the mundane / judge by what the world becomes if it's reliable*.
  - **Non-fungible, so equally safe:** the perk determines which competences were skipped; the player doesn't nominate them. **Not a discount** — the perk still costs full price.
  - **Needs no new rule:** it's the existing permission to *sell off free-package skills a character plausibly lacks* (`creating-characters.md` → Tweaking), which has had no rationale attached until now. Add one.
  - **Guards:** must not harden into *beautiful people are stupid*; must stay an invitation, never a GM-levied tax. Gaps stay narrow and non-crippling — anything genuinely impairing is a **flaw**.
- [x] ~~**Umbrella term**~~ **settled: "quirk"** (Tonio, 2026-08 — *"'Trait' is just too vague. It tends to apply to personality more than ability."*). *Quirk* is both the umbrella and the middle carrier; name the three explicitly — *perks, quirks and flaws* — wherever scope matters. Book chapter is **Quirks**. Still open: **talent vs perk**, two names for one carrier.
- [ ] **Add *Flaw* as a sixth carrier** (Tonio, 2026-08: "we have a category for outright bummers... like blindness"). It is ForePlay 2015's own term. ForePlay paid 1 attribute point / 2 skill points for a flaw **and apologised for it in the same breath** (*"this isn't intended to be a store... sometimes a weakness is just a weakness"*) — 2026 keeps the sentence and drops the grant. Touches the carrier table, `creating-characters.md` Step 3, and the character sheet (which currently has only `quirk[]`).
- [ ] **Label the quirk *families* explicitly** — Appearance, Empathy, Coordination, Endurance, Age — each with its combine rule. **A family is territory, not cost shape: one family spans perks, quirks and flaws** (Endurance = Kalenjin-runner genes / sprinter-vs-marathoner / a condition that guts EN). Default **best-applicable-only within a family** (same as tools/armor); exception where two members fire for *different* reasons (Appearance *Gorgeous* + Appearance *Scarred* can both apply — they're not two ends of one dial). The at-table test: *do these fire on this roll for the same reason?*
- [ ] **Endurance quirk family** (Tonio, 2026-08 — *EN keeps its attribute column*; see the worked family in the Design Document for why EN and AP resolve differently). EN bundles three poorly-correlated things, so the family should separate them. Every member attaches to the **exhaustion track** — rolls already being made.
  - *Sprinter* / *Marathoner* — explosive output vs sustained. **Same axis, mutually exclusive**: the clean demonstration of best-applicable-only.
  - *Iron Gut* — disease, poison, spoiled food, bad water. **Fires for a different reason than Marathoner, so the two stack** — the unfit man with a cast-iron stomach is an ordinary person, not an exploit.
  - *Cold-blooded* / *Heat-hardy* — environmental hardiness; likewise independent of cardio.
  - *Quick Recovery* / *Slow to Mend* — touches **clearing** exhaustion levels, not acquiring them. Mechanically distinct from every other member and cheap to run.
  - *Second Wind* — intermittent; triggers off carrying an exhaustion level, never its own timer.
  - *Short Sleeper* / *Needs His Nine Hours* — bites on recovery and on long operations.
- [ ] **Chronic conditions** (Tonio, 2026-08) — quirk *or* flaw, decided by the management line — write the four-line template (attach / trigger / effect / management) into the quirks rules page, plus a starter set. It is deliberately a *template*, not a catalogue: the space is endless, so shipping the ability to write one in a minute beats shipping a list. **The management line decides the carrier** — ForePlay already said it: *"Myopic — can be corrected with technology (in which case it's a Quirk)."* Managed → quirk; unmanageable in this setting → flaw. Worked examples to include:
  - **Haemophilia** — *attach:* wounds and convalescence rolls. *Trigger:* any wound. *Effect:* −2 EF convalescence; a wound that would stop on its own doesn't — first aid, or it worsens a level. *Management:* clotting factor at TL5; at TL1 you likely didn't reach adulthood. **The clearest demonstration that the same four lines describe a different life in a different setting.**
  - **Asthma** — *attach:* exhaustion checks. *Trigger:* cold air, dust, smoke, hard exertion. *Effect:* −1 EF on a check already being made; in a trigger environment, exertion that costs others nothing costs you a check. *Management:* an inhaler — one small losable object the whole table understands.
  - **Diabetes (insulin-dependent)** — *attach:* exhaustion track. *Trigger:* a missed meal; no insulin. *Effect:* off schedule, an exhaustion level; without insulin, a level a day that will not rest off. *Management:* insulin **and refrigeration** — the cold chain is the real constraint, and a wilderness or a blackout is therefore a plot.
  - **Chronic pain / an old break** — *attach:* Pain Resistance and agility tasks. *Trigger:* cold, damp, or the first hard exertion of the day. Shares the aging-quirk shape.
  - **Epilepsy**, **severe allergy** — same template; both want a *management* line and a trigger already on the table.
- [ ] **Severity = write it twice.** Mild and severe asthma are two entries, not one with a dial — and often the mild one is a quirk while the severe one is a flaw. Applies to the aging quirks too.
- [ ] **Acquired conditions** — a wound that heals badly becomes a chronic quirk — or a flaw if it's bad enough — via the same template. Cross-reference from `damage.md` recovery; this is the honest replacement for a scar table.
- [ ] **Test the featurelessness hypothesis before touching EN's column.** The critique is that designs don't differ on EN. If the cause is that EN has nothing to *say* — no quirks, no visible content in the builder — then the family is the fix and deleting the attribute would be treating the symptom. Add the family, then look at whether designs differ. Cheaper and more reversible than removing an axis.
- [ ] **Write Appearance quirks that name their audience.** The point of the family over the attribute is specificity: *striking in a particular way that doesn't work for everyone* is expressible as a quirk and not as a number. Observers agree at the extremes and diverge on type; the quirk should carry that, e.g. *Striking (to those who go for it)* vs *Conventionally handsome* vs *Imposing* vs *Forgettable*.
- [x] ~~**AP/EN removal**~~ **DONE 2026-08 — attributes are now five: ST CO IN PC WP** (Tonio: *"ST and CO are power and finesse of body, WP and IN power and finesse of mind, PC the feedback loop"*). EN's rolls → **WP**; poison/disease → **ST**; both supply quirk families. Applied to `character-rules.ts` (ATTRS, ARCH), `background-factors.json` (7 factors had their EN/AP grants converted to named quirks in `confers`), `creating-characters.md`, `character-sheet-paper.md`, `damage.md`, `exhaustion.md`, `gm-cheat-sheet.md`, `CLAUDE.md`. Skills needed no change — **EN already drove zero formulas.**
  - [x] ~~`playtest-pack.md`~~ done — five attributes, archetypes re-based to **40 points across five (each 6–10, avg 8)**, kept in sync with `ARCH` in `character-rules.ts`. Natural Athlete and Artistic now grant 2 points + a quirk. **The 40 is a re-derivation of the old 50-across-seven, not a calibration** — verify in playtest.
  - [ ] **The named quirks now referenced by background factors don't exist yet** — Hardy, Plain, Conditioned, Hardened, Weathered, Deep Reserves, Well-turned-out, Striking. Write them with the families.
  - **Why five and not six** (Tonio, 2026-08): the load reduction is **geometric, not linear**. Sinks go 9→5 (−44%), but *pairwise tradeoffs* — what a player actually weighs — go **36→10** via `n(n−1)/2`, a **3.6× cut**. Each removed axis takes a whole row and column of the comparison matrix. Corollary: with five axes each gets far more attention, so a non-discriminating attribute is conspicuous rather than merely inert.
  - [x] ~~**Beats divisor was a gotcha**~~ **FIXED 2026-08** (Tonio spotted it). `Score ÷ 5` primary / `÷ 10` secondary, round up, is a **breakpoint rule in formula's clothing** — Firearms 20 gives 4 beats and 21 gives 5, so the player who's read closely buys the 21st point. Replaced with **repetitions on the existing attention ladder**: every beat past the first is −1 (−2 secondary), doing exactly one thing is +1, and there is no cap. Deleted the divisor, the rounding, **and** the "attribute counts double" patch that existed only to prop the divisor up. Low-skill characters can now burn a magazine and hit nothing — self-limiting by outcome instead of by rule.
  - [x] ~~**Aim and movement were free actions**~~ **FIXED 2026-08** (Tonio). **Aim** is now a fire action that points the weapon: same roll, same modifiers, **advantage = `5 − QR`** (+4 at QR1 down to −2 at QR7), **QR10 ends that line of action for the round**. Recoil is an aim taken under recoil, so rate of fire self-regulates physically. **Movement** is a Coordination task: ground sets the EF, **QR7 loses balance, QR10 puts you down**; decline to roll and you simply move slowly at GM discretion.
  - **Why it matters** (Tonio): *a complete klutz could combat-move in melee exactly as well as an acrobat in the original rules*, and backing off and tripping over something — a staple of action cinema and of real fights — **never happened at anybody's table in any system**. Same blind spot as the chargen gotchas: **universal conventions don't get reported**. Two instances found; hunt for more deliberately.
  - **General move worth reusing:** replace a free/automatic action with an ordinary roll whose **QR produces the next state**. No new subsystem, and it gives **QR10** a consistent job everywhere — *no further action of this kind this round* — which is what removed the need for a cap on beats.
  - [ ] **Check `5 − QR` against `PM = 4 − QR`.** Aim is deliberately one step more generous than the standard performance modifier. Confirm that's intended and not drift.
  - [ ] **Calibrate the new slope.** Real nerf at the top: 5 beats was free at Score 25, now −4, which at *+1 EF ≈ your Score* is huge. Test (a) whether −1/beat is the right slope, and (b) whether **repeating one task should be cheaper than splitting between two** — firing twice is intuitively easier than firing while moving, and the merged ladder prices them identically. Prefer a gentler single slope over a second ladder.
  - **Behavioural evidence, 1986** (Tonio, 2026-08): chargen gotchas meant **new players' sheets were routinely rebuilt in the first session or two**. Nobody complained *because it was par for the course for RPGs of the era* — the cost was **invisible, not absent**. Two consequences: (a) **absence of complaint isn't evidence of absence** where the problem is genre-normal — added as a second qualifier to the evidence standard; (b) the corner-case fun was real but **regressively distributed**, accruing to the system-literate and paid for by newcomers.
  - **Design rule that falls out: put the depth where a mistake is cheap.** A bad decision in play costs a roll; in construction it costs the campaign. Construction forgiving and legible; **play** is where the system may be deep (EF ladder, reading a combat, finding the precise magical effect).
  - [ ] **Make rebuilding a stated right, not a GM favour** — it was universal in practice, i.e. the table was applying a social remedy to a mechanical fault. Say in `creating-characters.md` that a character may be freely rebuilt until the player is happy; the web sheet already makes it free.
  - **No mandatory purchases** (Tonio, 2026-08: *"you don't need to fine-tune a bunch of random other skills without which you're actually useless"*). **If a build is non-functional without X, X isn't a choice — it's a tax.** Make it free, fold it into the skill that implies it, or make it an attribute roll. Already applied piecemeal: Pain Resistance/Stamina aren't skills, Search and Initiative are in the free package, surgery folded into Repair. **Same rule as the power-pool access guard.** Audit the skill list against it once the list settles.
  - [ ] **Leverage watch:** CO gates pre-emption + all former DX/AG; WP now gates every exhaustion check, Pain Resistance and convalescence. WP's load is defensive (durability, not capability), which should contain it — verify in playtesting.
- [ ] **~~AP is not yet removed from the attribute list.~~** It drives nothing now, but deleting it before the quirk family exists would leave looks modelled by *nothing at all*. Sequence: write the AP quirk family, then drop AP from `ATTRS`/`ARCH`/`creating-characters.md`/the sheet — attributes become **6**.

## Planned: knowledge acquisition driven by IN + WP quirks (2026-08, Tonio)
**There is no Fields of Knowledge one-pager yet** — Fields appear only in passing in `skills.md` and the playtest pack, though `fields.json` (26 fields, hierarchical: `name/parent/years/thread`) exists. This is the spec for that page.

- **IN drives speed.** Decide what you want to learn and IN determines how fast you get there. (1986 had IN gate the *acquisition roll* at the end of study; the new emphasis is on rate.)
- **WP supplies monomania — via quirks, not a formula.** A WP-flavoured quirk lets you pour effort into one field to the neglect of others. Note this is exactly the "would the drawback bite?" test from the Design Document: someone taking *Monomaniacal* has already decided not to care about breadth, so the downside may be **free** — meaning it's a **perk** (paid for) rather than a quirk (traded). Price accordingly.
- **WP quirks also grant retries** — failing an acquisition roll isn't final if you're stubborn enough. Cheap and characterful, and it hangs off a roll that's already being made.
- Consistent with the rebalance: IN gains **direct load** (knowledge rate) on top of its formula load, and WP gets its effect **through quirks with stronger effects** rather than through a formula term.
- [ ] Open: does IN reduce the **years** (which changes cost, at 4 pts/year) or improve the **roll**, or both? Years is the powerful lever and affects the point economy directly.

## Open: do EN and AP earn their columns? (2026-08, Brett Evill + Tonio)
**The critique:** character designs don't tend to differ in EN and WP, and an attribute that drives no skills gets ignored by players. Proposal: reduce EN and AP to **quirks giving situational modifiers** — AP because attraction is audience-dependent anyway, EN because it's really just an EF modifier on avoiding exhaustion.

**Measured load** (skills.json, 20 skills; core = damage/exhaustion/scores/initiative):

| Attr | Skills driven | Core refs |
|---|--:|--:|
| PC | **14** | — |
| CO | 13 | — |
| IN | 6 | — |
| AP | 3 | 1 |
| ST | 2 | — |
| WP | **0** | 9 |
| EN | **0** | 10 |

*(Updated after the 2026-08 rebalance below. Originally: CO 14, IN 4, WP 1.)*

**Three findings the numbers force:**
1. **EN drives zero skills** — the critique is empirically correct.
2. **But WP drives exactly one.** EN and WP have the *same profile*: no skill load, carried entirely by core-rules load. This is **not** a close call between them — on this evidence they stand or fall together, and "drives no skills so players ignore it" condemns both or neither.
3. **AP is the opposite shape** (3 skills, 1 core ref) — genuinely skill-driven, and its effect really is situational (does this audience care?). It's the strongest quirk candidate of the three.

**⚠ Correction: skill-formula count is a poor proxy for attribute load.** The table above measures *formula load* only, and that distorts every case. **ST** shows 2 skills but also drives **unarmed damage, melee damage and carrying capacity** — its real weight is far above its footprint (which is why Melee at `(ST+CO)/2` is fine as-is: ST is paid twice, once in the Score and again in the damage). **EN and WP** show 0 but gate exhaustion checks, Pain Resistance and willpower rolls. **CO and PC** look dominant partly because formula load is *all* they have.

So there are two kinds of load: **formula load** (appears in a skill Score) and **direct load** (drives damage, capacity, resistance). The critique measures only the first.

**A better hypothesis for why players ignore EN — visibility, not worthlessness.** Formula load is **visible at character-design time**: buy a point of CO and numbers move on the sheet in front of you. Direct load is invisible until play — you don't see EN's value until the night you're still standing. Players optimise what the build screen shows them. If that's the mechanism, the remedy isn't amputation, it's **surfacing derived effects in the builder** — show ST's unarmed damage and carry capacity, EN's exhaustion threshold, WP's resistance, right beside the attribute as you buy it. That's a presentation fix for a problem currently being diagnosed as a rules fix.
- [ ] **Test this before deciding:** would a build screen that displays direct load change how testers allocate? Cheap to try, and it discriminates between "EN is worthless" and "EN is invisible".

**The framework gap this exposes.** The Coordination note tests *independence* — is this a separate capability, or a deviation from a correlated default? EN passes that easily (endurance athletes aren't strong; see the worked case). But independence is **necessary and not sufficient**: an attribute must also **discriminate between characters in play**. A capability can be genuinely independent *and* still be better modelled as a quirk, if nearly every character sits at the default. That's the same reasoning that folded Acrobatics — and it needs adding to the test.

**⚠ But check the cause before amputating.** CO and PC drive **14 of 20 skills each**; CO also gates pre-emption. If designs don't differentiate on EN, the likelier cause is that two attributes soak up almost all mechanical relevance and everything else is rounding error. The remedy might be **rebalancing what drives what** (does Melee really key off CO rather than ST+CO? should endurance gate anything a player wants to be *good* at?) rather than deleting attributes that are doing real work in the exhaustion system.
- [x] ~~**First rebalance applied (2026-08, Tonio)**~~ — **Confidence** `(AP+WP)/2` → **`(AP+IN)/2`**, and **Heavy Weapons** `(CO+PC)/2` → **`(IN+PC)/2`** (ranging and observation, not fine motor control). WP is now **deliberately out of every skill formula**, remaining as a *situational modifier* — arguably with stronger effects than it had as a formula term. Net: CO 14→13, IN 4→6, WP 1→0.
- **Consequence to note:** EN and WP now both drive **zero** skills, which is exactly the pair Brett observed designs don't differ on — but now *by design*. They're a distinct category: **core-resolution attributes** (exhaustion checks, Pain Resistance, willpower rolls) that never appear in a skill Score. Whether that category should exist at all, or collapse to quirks, is the live question.
- Also note **Melee is already `(ST+CO)/2`**, so brute-force-vs-elegant already works; ST's problem isn't missing from formulae, it's that it never appears *without* CO.
- [ ] **Decide in this order:** (a) is the CO/PC concentration itself the bug? (b) if EN goes, does WP go with it — and if not, why not, given identical profiles? (c) AP as a situational quirk is separable from both and could go first.

## Planned: aging quirks (2026-08)
- **Age should confer quirks**, in the two shapes above: *standing* (you've gained a few kilos — −EF on agility tasks and running speed; stiffness; failing eyesight) and *intermittent* (arthritis in cold or damp; an old break that aches on the first hard exertion; a knee that goes when you're already exhausted).
- **Acquired, not purchased.** These arrive with time and downtime — the same route by which background factors can be conferred after creation. A player doesn't shop for them.
- **They grant no points.** Per the rejected disadvantage economy: infirmity isn't currency. Which is fine, because ageing already pays in the other direction — years buy **fields, skills, contacts and background factors**. The real trade of age is *capability for capacity*, and it's fiction, not a ledger. A 55-year-old with four more BFs and a bad knee is a better character than a 25-year-old, not a worse one.
- **Intermittent must be trigger-driven, never timer-driven** (see Design Document) — hang it on weather, exertion, or an exhaustion level, so it costs no bookkeeping.
- Open: does age also gate *attribute* decline, or is that entirely quirk-borne? Quirk-borne is cheaper and more specific, and avoids a character sheet that quietly rots. Recommend quirks only.

## Planned: quirks for asymmetric ability
- **Live community pushback on the DX+AG→CO merge** (2026-08; no complaints about dropping inherent/trained). Counter-argument: splitting CO is the same shape as splitting PC into individual senses → complexity explosion. **Precedent worth citing:** 1986's own Abilities & Limitations table already handles senses as *traits* (superb/inferior hearing, superb/inferior eyesight, good sense of direction/time/distance), each a narrow BEF modifier to named skills — so "fine grain lives in traits, not attributes" is the original's own call, not a new one.
- **Quirks run both ways, but drawbacks are NOT a point source.** A negative quirk carries at most a *minor* offset — the GURPS/HERO disadvantage economy is rejected outright, because it makes character-building a minimax exercise (take blindness, fund telepathy) and because ForeSight tried it long ago with worse results than the commercial versions, having defined it in the most general form. So the deft-but-clumsy character is buildable and cheap, but banks no points. *(Corrected 2026-08 — an earlier version of this item asserted the merge required negative quirks to grant points; that imported a GURPS assumption.)*
- **Diagnosis of the pushback (Tonio):** notably *less* complaint about EM→PC. Likely because DX/AG attachment is a hangover from when **AG fed Speed and Speed drove the combat action economy** — AG was the optimization lever, EM had little mechanical leverage. So the objection tracks lost *leverage*, not lost expressiveness. **The strong reply:** that lever is already detached — modern Initiative is awareness/position-driven rather than derived from a Speed stat (deliberately, so ambush bites and waiting persists).
- **⚠ The mirror risk — leverage concentration.** Merging two leveraged stats can yield one double-leveraged stat: **CO** now gates pre-emption (it *is* the pre-emption roll) *plus* everything DX and AG separately gated *plus* the skill formulas that averaged them. Watch that CO doesn't become the obvious max-me attribute — the same optimization pathology, relocated. Mitigations already in place: escalating attribute cost past 12, and cheap narrow quirks reducing the pressure to max anything. **Check explicitly during point-economy playtesting.**
- **Way to settle it empirically:** ask objectors for character concepts CO+quirks genuinely can't express. The ones that survive are the quirks to write first.
- [ ] **Build out quirks to cover asymmetric dexterity/agility** — e.g. *graceful*, *nimble-fingered* — so a character can buy a **specific** excellent ability. Design intent: getting a really good *specific* ability should be **more affordable than maxing out a whole attribute**. This is the pressure-valve that makes the 7-attribute merge (DX+AG→CO, EM→PC) work: the fine grain moves into quirks rather than into more attributes.
